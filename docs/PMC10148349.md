## Abstract

Most biomedical information extraction (IE) approaches focus on entity types such as diseases, drugs, and genes, and relations such as gene-disease associations. This paper introduces the task of **methodological IE** to support fine-grained quality assessment of randomized controlled trial (RCT) publications. The authors draw from the Ontology of Clinical Research (OCRe) and the CONSORT reporting guidelines for RCTs to create a categorization of relevant methodological characteristics. In a pilot annotation study, they annotate a corpus of 70 full-text publications with these characteristics. They also train baseline named entity recognition (NER) models to recognize these items in RCT publications using several training sets with different negative sampling strategies. Models are evaluated at span and document levels. Results show that it is feasible to use NLP and machine learning for fine-grained extraction of methodological information. The annotated corpus, models, and code are publicly available.

## Problem Statement

Evidence synthesis from RCT publications relies on "risk of bias" assessment -- evaluating methodological quality characteristics such as randomization, blinding, and allocation concealment. This process is **largely manual and labor-intensive**. While biomedical NLP is increasingly used for extracting topical information (diseases, drugs, genes), research on extracting **methodological aspects** of studies remains scarce.

Existing NLP work on methodology has focused primarily on:
- PICO elements (Participants, Interventions, Comparison, Outcomes) -- which do not address methodological quality
- Coarse-grained sentence-level classification for risk of bias (e.g., RobotReviewer)

There is a gap in **fine-grained, span-level extraction** of methodological characteristics that could support transparent, evidence-based quality assessment. Methodological weaknesses can render a study's claims invalid, as illustrated by the ivermectin/COVID-19 debacle.

## Key Contributions

1. **Data model for methodological characteristics:** Developed a comprehensive, fine-grained data model drawn from OCRe and CONSORT guidelines, covering Trial Design, Blinding, Randomization, Sample Size, Settings, and Allocation Concealment, with hierarchical subtypes and properties.
2. **Annotated corpus:** Created a pilot corpus of 70 full-text RCT publications annotated at the span level with 1,734 annotations across all methodological categories, achieving high inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa 0.74--0.83; F1 0.90--0.94).
3. **Baseline NER models:** Trained and evaluated PubMedBERT-based NER models (token classification and CRF-based) with four negative sampling strategies, demonstrating feasibility of automated extraction.
4. **Best model performance:** CRF-based model with similarity sampling achieved 0.55 strict F1 / 0.66 partial F1 at span level and 0.90 F1 at document level.

## Methodology / Architecture

### Data Model

The data model captures methodological characteristics organized into six domains:

| Domain | Top-Level Categories | Subtypes (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trial Design** | Trial Design Type, Trial Phase, Comparative Intent | Parallel Group, Crossover, Factorial, N-of-1; Phase 1-4; Equivalence, Superiority, Non-inferiority |
| **Blinding** | Blinding Type, Blinding Object, Allocation Concealment Method | Open Label, Single-Blind, Double-Blind, Triple-Blind, Quadruple-Blind; Patients, Care Providers, Investigators, Outcome Assessors |
| **Randomization** | Randomization Type, Randomization Ratio, Randomization Personnel, Sequence Generation Method | Simple, Stratified (+ Criteria), Block (+ Size), Minimization (+ Criteria) |
| **Sample Size** | Sample Size Type, Power, Alpha, Dropout Rate | Required, Target, Actual at Enrollment, Actual at Outcome Analysis |
| **Settings** | Setting Type, Location | Single Center, Multicenter |
| **Allocation Concealment** | Allocation Concealment Method | -- |

### Annotation Process

- **Corpus:** 70 full-text RCT articles (25 from CONSORT-TM corpus + 45 from PubMed Central Open Access Subset)
- **Three phases:** (1) 10 articles by 3 annotators for calibration, (2) 20 articles by 3 annotators for IAA measurement, (3) 40 articles split between 2 annotators
- **Annotation tool:** brat
- **Approach:** Minimal annotation (shortest meaningful spans), focused on Abstracts, Methods, and Results sections
- **Automatic augmentation:** After manual annotation, all instances of annotated mentions were auto-labeled within the same section, then manually reviewed

### NER Models

- **Encoder:** PubMedBERT (base-uncased-abstract-fulltext)
- **Classification layers:** (a) Fully-connected token classification, (b) CRF-based sequence labeling
- **Tag scheme:** BIO
- **Four negative sampling strategies** to address extreme class imbalance (most sentences have no annotations):
  1. **Positive sentences only** -- only sentences with annotations
  2. **Random sampling** -- positive + one random negative per positive
  3. **Similarity sampling** -- positive + most similar negative (by PubMedBERT cosine similarity)
  4. **Random+Similarity** -- combination of approaches 2 and 3
- **Hyperparameters:** Batch size 4, Adam optimizer, learning rates {1e-5, 2e-5, 3e-5, 5e-5}, epochs {10, 20, 30}
- **Best settings:** LR 5e-5 for token classification, LR 3e-5 for CRF, 20 epochs for both
- **Split:** 55 articles for training, 15 for testing

### Per-Characteristic Performance (Best Model, Span Level, Partial F1)

- **Perfect:** Trial Phase (1.00)
- **High (>0.80):** Randomization Ratio (0.97), Power (0.95), Alpha (0.86), Stratification Criteria (0.81), Crossover Period/Treatment (0.80)
- **Moderate (0.50--0.80):** Blinding Type (0.78), Sequence Generation (0.77), Settings Type (0.74), Minimization Criteria (0.71), Sample Size Type (0.63)
- **Low (<0.50):** Blinding Objects (0.43), Randomization Personnel (0.20), Block Size (0.50), Allocation Concealment Methods (0.00)

## Limitations

1. **Small corpus size:** Only 70 annotated articles; the corpus needs expansion for broader utility and improved model training.
2. **Baseline models only:** Only experimented with basic PubMedBERT + token classification and CRF layers; more advanced architectures (e.g., BiLSTM+CRF) were not explored.
3. **Limited hyperparameter tuning:** Only learning rate was systematically explored.
4. **Rare categories:** Some categories (e.g., Allocation Concealment Method with only 12 instances) have too few examples for reliable training.
5. **Implicit mentions:** Some characteristics are implicit rather than explicit (e.g., Parallel Group design inferred from intervention descriptions), making annotation and extraction more challenging.
6. **No relation extraction:** The study focuses on NER only; the relationships between extracted entities (e.g., linking a block size to its randomization type) are not modeled.

## Relevance to CTRA

This paper is directly relevant to CTRA's goal of predicting protocol uninformativeness:

- **Methodological quality signals as features:** The fine-grained methodological characteristics extracted by this approach (blinding type, randomization method, sample size calculations, allocation concealment) are strong indicators of trial quality and could serve as **input features for CTRA's uninformativeness prediction model**. Poorly designed protocols (e.g., lacking proper blinding, inadequate randomization, missing sample size justification) are more likely to produce uninformative results.
- **Protocol quality assessment:** CTRA aims to assess whether a trial protocol will yield useful results. The presence or absence of key methodological elements (as defined in this data model) directly relates to the risk that a trial will be uninformative. For example, missing allocation concealment or inadequate blinding increases risk of bias, which can render results uninterpretable.
- **Automated feature extraction pipeline:** The NER models developed here could be integrated into CTRA's preprocessing pipeline to automatically extract methodological features from protocol text and published trial reports. The document-level F1 of 0.90 suggests practical utility.
- **Data model as a schema:** The OCRe/CONSORT-based data model provides a structured vocabulary for characterizing trial methodology that could be adopted as part of CTRA's feature schema.
- **Complementary to HINT:** While HINT (paper [10]) focuses on drug/disease/criteria features for outcome prediction, this paper provides the methodology-quality dimension that HINT lacks. CTRA could combine both approaches -- using HINT-style multi-modal features alongside methodological quality features from this work.
- **Important distinction:** This paper extracts methodological characteristics from **published papers** (post-hoc), whereas CTRA operates on **protocols** (pre-trial). However, many of the same methodological elements (randomization, blinding, sample size) are specified in protocols and could be extracted with adapted models.
