## Abstract

Learning exists in the context of data, yet notions of confidence typically focus on model predictions, not label quality. Confident learning (CL) is a data-centric approach which focuses instead on label quality by characterizing and identifying label errors in datasets, based on the principles of pruning noisy data, counting with probabilistic thresholds to estimate noise, and ranking examples to train with confidence. Building on the assumption of a class-conditional noise process, CL directly estimates the joint distribution between noisy (given) labels and uncorrupted (unknown) labels. This results in a generalized CL which is provably consistent and experimentally performant. The authors present sufficient conditions where CL exactly finds label errors, show CL performance exceeding seven recent competitive approaches for learning with noisy labels on CIFAR, find label errors in presumed error-free MNIST and ImageNet datasets, and improve sentiment classification on Amazon Reviews text data. Results are replicable using the open-source cleanlab framework.

---

## Problem Statement

Large datasets with noisy labels have become increasingly common, spanning prominent benchmarks like ImageNet and MS-COCO to human-centric datasets like electronic health records. The presence of noisy labels introduces two problems: (1) How can we identify examples with label errors? (2) How can we learn well despite noisy labels, irrespective of the data modality or model employed? Most prior approaches are model-centric (introducing new models or loss functions). This paper instead takes a data-centric approach, directly estimating the joint distribution of noisy and true labels to find and remove label errors before training.

---

## Key Contributions

1. **Theoretical framework:** Proved realistic sufficient conditions under which CL exactly finds label errors and exactly estimates the joint distribution of noisy and true labels (Theorems 1 and 2), even with noisy predicted probabilities.
2. **Confident Joint data structure:** Introduced the confident joint (C_{y~,y*}), an unnormalized count matrix that partitions examples by their noisy and estimated true labels using per-class thresholds, providing robustness to class imbalance and heterogeneous probability distributions.
3. **Joint distribution estimation:** Directly estimated the full m x m joint distribution Q_{y~,y*} between noisy labels and true labels, from which noise rates, inverse noise rates, and latent priors can all be derived.
4. **Five rank-and-prune methods** for finding and removing label errors: C_confusion, C_{y~,y*}, Prune by Class (PBC), Prune by Noise Rate (PBNR), and C+NR.
5. **Empirical validation:** Outperformed seven competitive methods (INCV, Mixup, SCE-loss, MentorNet, Co-Teaching, S-Model, Reed) on CIFAR-10 across varying noise levels and sparsity.
6. **Real-world label error discovery:** Identified label errors in presumed error-free MNIST, ImageNet (ILSVRC 2012), WebVision, and Amazon Reviews datasets; demonstrated ontological issue discovery in ImageNet (e.g., duplicate maillot class, projectile/missile confusion).
7. **Open-source implementation:** Released the cleanlab Python package at [https://github.com/cleanlab/cleanlab/](https://github.com/cleanlab/cleanlab/).

## Methodology/Architecture

### Core Assumptions
- **Class-conditional noise process (CNP):** Label noise depends only on the latent true class, not the data features. I.e., p(y~|y*; x) = p(y~|y*).
- **Model-agnostic:** CL works with any model that produces predicted probabilities (neural networks, logistic regression, etc.).

### Three-Step CL Procedure

**Step 1: Count -- Estimate the Confident Joint C_{y~,y*}**
- Compute out-of-sample predicted probabilities P_hat (via k-fold cross-validation).
- For each class j, compute a per-class threshold t_j = average self-confidence of examples labeled j.
- An example x labeled i is counted into bin (i, j) of C_{y~,y*} if its predicted probability for class j exceeds t_j and j is the argmax among classes exceeding their thresholds.
- Label collisions (example counted into multiple bins) are resolved by argmax.
- The joint Q_hat is estimated by calibrating C_{y~,y*} so that row sums match the observed noisy label marginals and the total sums to 1.

**Step 2: Prune -- Remove label errors using one of five methods**
- CL baseline (C_confusion): Flag examples where given label differs from argmax prediction.
- CL: C_{y~,y*}: Use off-diagonal entries of the confident joint directly.
- PBC (Prune by Class): For each class, remove the n * Q_hat estimated errors with lowest self-confidence.
- PBNR (Prune by Noise Rate): For each off-diagonal entry, remove estimated errors by max margin.
- C+NR: Intersection of PBC and PBNR.

**Step 3: Train -- Retrain on cleaned data**
- Reweight the loss by Q_hat_{y*}[i] / Q_hat_{y~,y*}[i][i] for each class i to account for removed data.

### Theoretical Results
- **Theorem 1 (Exact Label Errors):** Under ideal predicted probabilities and diagonal-dominant noise matrix, CL exactly finds all label errors.
- **Corollary 1.1 (Per-Class Robustness):** CL is robust to per-class linear transformations of probabilities (unlike C_confusion baseline).
- **Theorem 2 (Per-Example Robustness):** CL exactly finds label errors even when every example's predicted probability has per-example error, as long as the error stays within a bounded range relative to the threshold.

### Complexity
- O(m^2 + nm) storage and arithmetic operations for n examples over m classes.

## Results

### CIFAR-10 (Synthetic Noise)
- CL methods consistently outperform all seven baselines across 20%, 40%, and 70% noise levels and 0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 sparsity.
- At 40% noise / 0.6 sparsity: CL: PBNR achieves **87.2%** test accuracy vs. INCV at 73.6%, Mixup at 59.8%, baseline at 57.3%.
- CL methods are robust across sparsity settings, while other methods degrade significantly with increasing sparsity.
- Joint estimation RMSE: 0.004 for 20% and 40% noise settings on average.
- Label error finding: CL achieves up to 90% accuracy, 79% F1, 70% precision, and 97% recall.

### ImageNet
- Identified label errors, multi-label images, and ontological issues (e.g., 645 projectile images mislabeled as missile, duplicate maillot class, bathtub/tub confusion).
- Human validation: 58% of CL-identified issues were confirmed as multi-label, ontological issues, or errors.
- Training ResNet-18/50 on CL-cleaned ImageNet (removing 10-20% of data) moderately improved validation accuracy, especially on noisy classes (e.g., foxhound: +6-13% improvement; maillot: +6-17% improvement).

### Amazon Reviews
- CL with logistic regression on fastText embeddings: all CL methods achieve ~90% test accuracy (50 epochs, 1M train) vs. 84.4% baseline.
- CL outperforms baseline even with nearly half the training data pruned.
- CL is robust to overfitting -- baseline accuracy drops with more epochs while CL accuracy continues to improve.

### MNIST
- Found blatant label errors in the original, unperturbed MNIST training set (e.g., a "7" labeled as "4", a "3" labeled as "5").

## Limitations

- The class-conditional noise process assumption (label noise depends only on true class, not features) may not hold for all datasets, though it is reasonable for many.
- CL theory requires predicted probabilities to be reasonably accurate (within bounded error of ideal probabilities); extremely poor models may not satisfy the sufficient conditions.
- The per-example diffracted noise condition (Theorem 2) assumes uniformly distributed error, which may be an approximation.
- Discretization error exists when estimating real-valued joint distributions from discrete counts, especially with small class sizes.
- The paper primarily evaluates image classification and text classification; extension to seq2seq, regression, or structured prediction tasks is left as future work.
- CL does not prescribe a specific training procedure after cleaning; the retraining approach and its interaction with CL could be further optimized.

## Relevance to CTRA

This paper is highly relevant to the CTRA project in several important ways:

1. **Data quality for clinical trial labels:** The CTRA project predicts protocol uninformativeness. Labels for "uninformative" vs. "informative" clinical trials are inherently noisy -- expert annotators may disagree, definitions of uninformativeness may vary, and retrospective labeling is prone to error. Confident learning provides a principled framework for identifying and cleaning such noisy labels before training a predictive model.

2. **Class-conditional noise in clinical trial data:** The assumption that label noise is class-conditional (e.g., an uninformative trial is more likely to be mislabeled as informative than as a completely unrelated category) is reasonable for the CTRA binary or multi-class classification setting.

3. **Handling class imbalance:** Clinical trial datasets are likely class-imbalanced (many more informative trials than uninformative ones). CL's per-class thresholding explicitly addresses this, unlike simpler approaches that fail under imbalance.

4. **Model-agnostic data cleaning:** CL can be applied on top of any model the CTRA project uses (BERT, logistic regression, tree-based models, etc.) -- it only requires out-of-sample predicted probabilities.

5. **Joint distribution estimation for error characterization:** Understanding the patterns of label noise (e.g., which types of uninformative trials are most often mislabeled) could provide insights into the labeling process and help refine annotation guidelines.

6. **Text data applicability:** The Amazon Reviews experiments demonstrate CL works well on text classification with logistic regression on embeddings, which parallels the NLP-based approach CTRA might take with clinical trial protocol text.

7. **The cleanlab package** provides a readily usable implementation for integrating confident learning into the CTRA pipeline.
