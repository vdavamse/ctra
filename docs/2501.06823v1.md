## Abstract

Clinical trials are the gold standard for assessing the effectiveness and safety of drugs for treating diseases. Given the vast design space of drug molecules, elevated financial cost, and multi-year timeline of these trials, research on clinical trial outcome prediction has gained immense traction. Accurate predictions must leverage data of diverse modes such as drug molecules, target diseases, and eligibility criteria to infer successes and failures. Previous Deep Learning approaches for this task, such as HINT, often require wet lab data from synthesized molecules and/or rely on prior knowledge to encode interactions as part of the model architecture. MEXA-CTP is a lightweight attention-based model that integrates readily-available multi-modal data and generates effective representations via specialized modules dubbed "mode experts", while avoiding human biases in model design. Optimized with the Cauchy loss to capture relevant interactions across modes, MEXA-CTP improves upon existing approaches by up to 11.3% in F1 score, 12.2% in PR-AUC, and 2.5% in ROC-AUC compared to HINT on the Trial Outcome Prediction (TOP) benchmark.

## Problem Statement

Existing deep learning approaches for clinical trial outcome prediction (notably HINT) suffer from three key limitations:

1. **Paragraph-level embedding limitations**: HINT uses paragraph-level embeddings for trial protocols that do not distinguish between inclusion and exclusion criteria, potentially confusing the model. It also relies on ClinicalBERT with a sliding window, missing subtle nuances between sentences.
2. **Reliance on hard-to-acquire data**: Drug molecules in HINT are represented through estimated pharmacokinetics properties (ADMET) requiring extensive wet lab data, which is expensive and unavailable for unsynthesized molecules.
3. **Human biases in neural network design**: Manually designed module connections based on human intuition can limit the network's capacity and enforce biases.

## Key Contributions

1. Introduction of MEXA-CTP, a method that integrates multi-modal data via "mode experts" and is optimized with Cauchy and contrastive losses to capture drug-disease, drug-protocol, and disease-protocol interactions without hand-crafted structures.
2. Evaluation against several baselines including HINT on real-world Phase I, II, and III clinical trials, yielding up to 11.3% (F1), 12.2% (PR-AUC), and 2.5% (ROC-AUC) improvement over HINT.
3. Case study and ablation study demonstrating the contribution of key components to prediction power.

## Methodology/Architecture

MEXA-CTP consists of four stages:

### Stage 1: Encoding Module
- **Drug Molecules**: SMILES strings encoded using DeepChem; an embedding dictionary is built for SMILES segments for efficiency.
- **Target Diseases**: ICD-10 codes mapped to embeddings using the `icdcodex` package.
- **Eligibility Criteria**: Inclusion and exclusion criteria are split at the statement level and separately encoded using BioBERT, with [CLS] and [SEP] tokens.

### Stage 2: Knowledge Embedding Module
- Drug molecules and target disease embeddings are enriched via multi-layer transformer encoders.
- Eligibility criteria embeddings receive sinusoidal positional embeddings to capture statement order, then pass through a siamese-design multi-layer transformer encoder.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria outputs are concatenated.

### Stage 3: Mode Experts Module
Three mode experts model interactions between drug-disease, drug-criteria, and disease-criteria pairs:
- **Token Selection**: A projection layer predicts token selection probability. Hard margin masks tokens below threshold `t`; soft margin modulates tokens by their confidence. A **Cauchy loss** regularizes token selection to focus on necessary tokens.
- **Cross-Attention**: Mode experts exchange information via cross-attention, producing six interaction representations (Imd, Idm, Icd, Idc, Imc, Icm).
- **Self-supervised Learning**: NT-Xent contrastive loss treats reciprocal interaction pairs as positive samples and other combinations as negatives.

### Stage 4: Knowledge Compensation Module
- Self-attention encoder processes concatenated expert outputs.
- Average pooling across the sequence dimension, followed by a projection head with sigmoid for binary prediction.
- Class-weighted binary cross-entropy (wBCE) loss handles class imbalance.

### Combined Loss Function
`L = L_cls + lambda_1 * L_cauchy + lambda_2 * L_contrastive`

### Architecture Details
- 2 attention layers per block, 2 attention heads, embedding size 16, explosion size 32.
- Adam optimizer, batch size 64, learning rate 5e-2.
- Training complexity: O(d * n^2), where d=32 and n <= 46.

## Results

### Main Results (averages over 10 bootstrap samples on 80% of test)

| Phase | Method    | F1            | PR-AUC        | ROC-AUC       |
|-------|-----------|---------------|---------------|---------------|
| I     | HINT      | .598+/-.011   | .581+/-.021   | .573+/-.024   |
| I     | MEXA-CTP  | **.713+/-.027** | **.605+/-.014** | **.593+/-.012** |
| II    | HINT      | .635+/-.011   | .607+/-.012   | .621+/-.015   |
| II    | MEXA-CTP  | **.695+/-.008** | **.635+/-.015** | **.638+/-.005** |
| III   | HINT      | .814+/-.013   | .603+/-.014   | .685+/-.023   |
| III   | MEXA-CTP  | **.857+/-.007** | **.771+/-.016** | **.693+/-.025** |

Average improvement over HINT: +11.3% F1, +12.2% PR-AUC, +2.5% ROC-AUC.

### Ablation Studies (Phase III)
- **Aggregation**: [CLS] token far outperforms Avg and Sum methods.
- **Positional Embedding**: Including PE improves all metrics.
- **Loss variants**: Full combined loss (wBCE + Cauchy + contrastive) outperforms any subset.
- **Token Selection**: Learned selection outperforms random selection; comparable to using all tokens but with better F1 and PR-AUC.

### Token Usage Analysis
- Disease and molecule experts show near-uniform token selection.
- Criteria expert preferentially selects earlier-indexed tokens, consistent with importance ordering in eligibility criteria text.

## Limitations

- The paper does not explicitly enumerate limitations in a dedicated section.
- Implicitly: the model is evaluated only on the TOP benchmark (a single dataset); generalization to other clinical trial datasets is not demonstrated.
- The model predicts binary trial outcomes (success/failure) and does not model degrees of success or nuanced outcomes.
- The approach still requires structured inputs (SMILES, ICD-10 codes, eligibility criteria text) and does not handle missing modalities.

## Relevance to CTRA

MEXA-CTP is directly relevant to the CTRA project (predicting protocol uninformativeness in clinical trials):

- **Eligibility criteria encoding**: The statement-level encoding of inclusion/exclusion criteria with positional embeddings and BioBERT could inform how CTRA encodes protocol text. The finding that statement order matters is relevant for protocol quality assessment.
- **Mode experts architecture**: The cross-attention mechanism between drug, disease, and criteria modalities provides a template for fusing heterogeneous clinical trial data in CTRA.
- **Token selection mechanism**: The Cauchy-loss-driven token selection identifies which parts of each modality are most informative, which could help CTRA identify uninformative or problematic protocol elements.
- **Benchmark and data**: CTRA could use the TOP benchmark as a secondary evaluation dataset, or adapt the multimodal fusion approach for risk assessment rather than outcome prediction.
- **Key distinction**: MEXA-CTP predicts whether a trial will succeed (drug efficacy/safety), whereas CTRA predicts whether a protocol is uninformative. However, the underlying data modalities (drugs, diseases, criteria) and the multimodal fusion challenges are shared.
