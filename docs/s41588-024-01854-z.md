## Abstract

Many drug discovery projects are started but few progress fully through clinical trials to approval. Previous work has shown that human genetics support for the therapeutic hypothesis increases the chance of trial progression. The authors applied natural language processing (NLP) to classify the free-text reasons for 28,561 clinical trials that stopped before their endpoints were met (withdrawn, terminated, or suspended in ClinicalTrials.gov). They then evaluated these classified stop reasons in light of the underlying evidence for the therapeutic hypothesis and target properties. Key findings: (1) trials are more likely to stop because of a lack of efficacy in the absence of strong genetic evidence from human populations or genetically modified animal models; (2) certain trials are more likely to stop for safety reasons if the drug target gene is highly constrained in human populations and if the gene is broadly expressed across tissues. These results support the growing use of human genetics to evaluate targets for drug discovery programs.

## Problem Statement

Clinical trial failure is the dominant outcome in drug development, with lack of efficacy and safety issues accounting for ~79% of setbacks. However, systematically understanding *why* trials fail is hampered by publication bias toward positive results and the lack of structured data on failure reasons. ClinicalTrials.gov provides free-text stop reasons for halted trials, but these have not been systematically classified at scale. Previous work manually classified only 3,125 stopped trials. The authors address this gap by scaling NLP-based classification to 28,561 stopped trials and linking the classified outcomes to genetic and genomic evidence to understand what factors predict different types of trial failure.

## Key Contributions

1. **Developed and validated a BERT-based NLP classifier** for clinical trial stop reasons that achieves F_micro = 0.91 in cross-validation and 0.70--0.83 on held-out data, classifying trials into 15 stop reasons grouped into 6 higher-level outcome categories.
2. **Classified 28,561 stopped clinical trials** from ClinicalTrials.gov (as of November 2021), creating the largest systematic catalog of trial stop reasons to date.
3. **Demonstrated that genetic support is protective against early trial stoppage:** stopped trials overall are depleted in genetic support (OR = 0.73, P = 3.4 x 10^-69^), and trials stopped for negative outcomes (lack of efficacy/futility) show the strongest depletion (OR = 0.61, P = 6 x 10^-18^).
4. **Identified target properties that predict safety-related stoppages:** highly constrained genes (high pLOEUF) carry 1.5x increased risk; loss-of-function intolerant genes (pLI > 0.9) carry 1.4x increased risk; broadly expressed genes carry 1.3x increased risk; tissue-enriched genes are protective (OR = 0.8).
5. **Revealed that enrollment-related stoppages also show genetic depletion** (OR = 0.61, P < 1 x 10^-40^), suggesting that lack of confidence in the therapeutic hypothesis may contribute to recruitment difficulties.
6. **Provided public datasets and models** for ongoing classification of stopped trials, updated quarterly through the Open Targets Platform.

## Methodology/Architecture

### NLP Classification Pipeline

1. **Training data:** Revisited the manual classification from Pak et al. (2015) of 3,124 stopped trials, plus 447 COVID-19 pandemic-related stops, totaling 3,571 manually classified studies across 17 stop reasons and 6 outcome categories.
2. **Semantic analysis:** Trained an LSTM network to create sentence embeddings for each stop reason. Computed cosine similarity and performed agglomerative hierarchical clustering to identify semantically similar classes. Merged similar low-frequency classes based on both clinical expertise and semantic similarity.
3. **Classification model:** Fine-tuned BERT (uncased, pre-trained) with a one-layer feed-forward classifier (50 hidden units, ReLU activation). Used the `[CLS]` token representation fed into a sigmoid classifier. Training: 7 epochs, batch size 32, learning rate 5 x 10^-5^, AdamW optimizer (weight decay 10^-2^). Treated as multi-label: top 3 predictions above a threshold.
4. **Validation:** Additional 1,675 manually curated stop reasons from randomly selected unseen studies. Inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa): 0.66--0.80 across 3 annotator pairs.

### Genetic Evidence Integration

- **Human genetic evidence:** 13 sources from Open Targets Platform (April 2022): OT Genetics Portal, Gene Burden (UK Biobank), ClinVar (germline + somatic), ClinGen Gene Validity, Genomics England PanelApp, Gene2Phenotype, Orphanet, UniProt, COSMIC, IntOGen.
- **Mouse models:** International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium (IMPC) -- phenocopying knockouts.
- **Ontology expansion:** Experimental Factor Ontology (EFO) used to harmonize indications; genetic evidence ontologically expanded, yielding 3,654,109 gene-trait pairs.

### Drug-Target Mapping

- Drug-to-target mapping via ChEMBL mechanism of action annotations.
- Resulting dataset: 594,375 clinical target-disease records, 71,419 unique target-disease associations, 57,775 in stopped studies.

### Target Property Annotations

- **Genetic constraint:** gnomAD pLOEUF quantiles (6 bins) and pLI scores.
- **Tissue specificity:** Human Protein Atlas baseline transcriptomics (tissue enriched, group enriched, tissue enhanced, low tissue specificity).
- **Network connectivity:** IntAct protein-protein interactions (MI score > 0.42 threshold), binned by number of partners.

### Statistical Approach

- Two-sided Fisher's exact test for all case-control comparisons.
- No multiple testing correction applied.
- All analyses performed with SciPy v1.11.4.

## Results

### Distribution of Stop Reasons

| Stop Reason | Percentage | Outcome Category |
|-------------|-----------|------------------|
| Insufficient enrollment | 36.67% | Neutral |
| Business or administrative | ~15% | Neutral |
| Logistics or resources | ~10% | Neutral |
| Negative (efficacy/futility) | 7.6% (2,197 trials) | Negative |
| Study design | ~4% | Suspicious |
| Safety or side effects | 3.38% (977 trials) | Safety |
| COVID-19 | ~5% | Neutral |
| Other reasons | Remaining | Various |

48% of stopped trials with a reason were indicated for oncology.

### Safety-Related Stoppages and Target Properties (All Indications)

| Feature | Category | OR (95% CI) | P value |
|---------|----------|-------------|---------|
| Genetic constraint (pLOEUF) | Very high | 1.47 (1.36--1.59) | 1.0 x 10^-22^ |
| Genetic constraint (pLOEUF) | Lowest | 0.65 (0.51--0.81) | 8.1 x 10^-5^ |
| LoF intolerance | pLI > 0.9 | 1.37 (1.27--1.48) | 4.8 x 10^-15^ |
| LoF intolerance | pLI < 0.1 | 0.82 (0.76--0.89) | 4.9 x 10^-7^ |
| RNA specificity | Low tissue specificity | 1.29 (1.19--1.39) | 1.6 x 10^-10^ |
| RNA specificity | Tissue enriched | 0.81 (0.72--0.90) | 1.8 x 10^-4^ |

| Interacting proteins | None reported | 0.64 (0.56--0.74) | 4.9 x 10^-11^ |

Safety-related signals are largely driven by oncology trials; non-oncology stratification shows similar trends but without statistical significance.

### Phase Distribution of Stop Reasons

- Negative outcomes enriched in Phase II (OR = 1.9, P = 2.4 x 10^-38^) and Phase III (OR = 2.6, P = 3.64 x 10^-55^).
- Safety/side effects decline after Phase I (OR = 2.4, P = 9.63 x 10^-23^).
- Staff relocation more frequent in Early Phase I.

## Limitations

1. **Off-target effects not captured:** Many trial failures may be explained by pharmacokinetics, drug delivery, toxicology, or off-target effects rather than target biology. These factors are not considered in the analysis.
2. **Reported reasons may be incomplete:** The free-text reasons submitted to ClinicalTrials.gov may represent only a fraction of the actual reasons contributing to the decision to halt a study. The unexpected depletion of genetic evidence in enrollment-related stoppages suggests hidden confounds.
3. **NLP model performance varies by class:** Strong for frequent/linguistically simple classes (e.g., insufficient enrollment F = 0.98, COVID-19 F = 1.00) but weaker for complex/ambiguous classes (e.g., another study F = 0.71, insufficient data -- lower performance).
4. **No multiple testing correction** was applied to Fisher's exact test P values.
5. **Ancestry bias:** Data sources (clinical curation of rare variants, GWAS) may present biases toward European ancestries.
6. **Stopped trials represent only a subset of failures:** Only 7.9% of trials that stop early do so via withdrawal, termination, or suspension. Completed but negative trials (the majority of failures) are not captured in this analysis.
7. **Scope limited to trials with known drug mechanisms of action:** Only drugs with ChEMBL-mapped targets were included.

## Relevance to CTRA

This paper is **highly relevant to CTRA** in multiple ways:

- **Complementary outcome taxonomy:** The CTRA project predicts protocol uninformativeness (broadly defined, encompassing design flaws, feasibility issues, and reporting failures). Ochoa et al. provide a complementary taxonomy of *why* trials stop, distinguishing negative (efficacy/futility), safety, and neutral/operational reasons. The 15-class NLP classification and 6 higher-level outcome categories could inform or refine CTRA's label definitions.

- **Largest labeled dataset of trial stop reasons:** The 28,561 classified stopped trials represent a valuable labeled dataset. CTRA could use these classified outcomes as auxiliary labels, additional features, or for validation -- e.g., testing whether CTRA's "uninformativeness" predictions correlate with specific Ochoa stop-reason categories.

- **Evidence that "neutral" stoppages also reflect weak hypotheses:** The finding that enrollment-related stoppages (classified as "neutral") show strong depletion of genetic evidence (OR = 0.61) supports CTRA's broader framing that protocol-level problems and therapeutic implausibility are intertwined.

- **Feature engineering inspiration:** The genetic evidence features (Open Targets genetic support, gnomAD constraint, tissue specificity, protein interaction network degree) could serve as input features for CTRA models, particularly for predicting safety-related or efficacy-related uninformativeness.

- **NLP methodology:** The BERT-based classification of free-text fields from ClinicalTrials.gov demonstrates feasibility of NLP on clinical trial metadata, applicable to CTRA's own text feature extraction from protocols and registry data.

- **Validation of ClinicalTrials.gov as data source:** The paper validates ClinicalTrials.gov stop reason data as a meaningful, analyzable signal for clinical trial outcome assessment.

- **Distinction from CTRA scope:** Importantly, Ochoa et al. focus on *why trials fail biologically* (drug target genetics, efficacy, safety), whereas CTRA focuses on *protocol-level uninformativeness* (design quality, feasibility, reporting). These are complementary perspectives: a trial can be uninformative (CTRA) even if the underlying therapeutic hypothesis is sound, and vice versa.
