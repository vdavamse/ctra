## Abstract

We introduce C-Pack, a package of resources that significantly advances the field of general text embeddings for Chinese. C-Pack includes three critical resources. 1) C-MTP is a massive training dataset for text embedding, which is based on the curation of vast unlabeled corpora and the integration of high-quality labeled corpora. 2) C-MTEB is a comprehensive benchmark for Chinese text embeddings covering 6 tasks and 35 datasets. 3) BGE is a family of embedding models covering multiple sizes. Our models outperform all prior Chinese text embeddings on C-MTEB by more than +10% upon the time of the release. We also integrate and optimize the entire suite of training methods for BGE. Along with our resources on general Chinese embedding, we release our data and models for English text embeddings. The English models also achieve state-of-the-art performance on the MTEB benchmark; meanwhile, our released English data is 2 times larger than the Chinese data.

## Problem Statement

General-purpose text embeddings — one model serving retrieval, ranking, classification and clustering across arbitrary domains — are harder to train than task-specific ones, and the paper identifies three simultaneous prerequisites: **data** at scale, an appropriate **encoder and training recipe**, and a **benchmark** broad enough to measure generality. English had BEIR and MTEB and several strong models (E5, GTE, GTR, OpenAI Ada-002); Chinese had neither comparable benchmarks nor comparable training data.

C-Pack's stated contribution is to supply all three at once for Chinese, and — because the same recipe was applied to an English corpus twice the size — to advance the English state of the art as a by-product.

## Key Contributions

1. **C-MTP** — a training dataset of ~100M text pairs from 16 sources, split into an *unlabeled* portion (curated from web-scale corpora) and a smaller *labeled* portion.
2. **C-MTEB** — a Chinese benchmark, 35 datasets across 6 task types, built as the Chinese extension of MTEB.
3. **BGE** — a family of BERT-architecture embedding models at three scales, released for both Chinese and English.
4. **A fully released three-stage training recipe**, with ablations isolating the contribution of each stage.

## Methodology / Architecture

### Model family

BERT-like architecture, three scales:

| Scale | Parameters | Chinese dim | **English dim** |
|---|---|---|---|
| small | **24M** | 512 | **384** |
| base | **102M** | 768 | 768 |
| large | **326M** | 1024 | 1024 |

The authors position the small model explicitly: "way faster and lighter, making it suitable to handle massive knowledge bases and high-throughput applications."

### Three-stage training recipe

**Stage 1 — Pre-training with plain text.** A RetroMAE-style masked auto-encoder objective: the polluted text is encoded into a single embedding, from which the clean text is recovered by a lightweight decoder.

```
min  Σ_{x ∈ X}  − log Dec(x | e_X̃),     e_X̃ ← Enc(X̃)
```

The point is to force all information needed for reconstruction through the embedding vector, preparing the encoder for the embedding task rather than for token-level prediction.

**Stage 2 — General-purpose contrastive fine-tuning on C-MTP (unlabeled).** Standard InfoNCE:

```
min  Σ_{(p,q)}  − log [ e^{⟨e_p, e_q⟩/τ} / ( e^{⟨e_p, e_q⟩/τ} + Σ_{Q'} e^{⟨e_p, e_q'⟩/τ} ) ]
```

**Critically: no hard-negative mining at this stage.** "Instead of mining hard negative samples on purpose, we purely rely on in-batch negative samples and resort to a big batch size (as large as **19,200**)."

**Stage 3 — Task-specific fine-tuning on C-MTP (labeled).** Two changes: (a) **instruction-based fine-tuning**, where a task-specific verbal instruction *I_t* is appended to the query side (`q' ← q + I_t`, e.g. "search relevant passages for the query"); and (b) **one mined hard negative per pair**, ANN-sampled from the task's own corpus, in addition to in-batch negatives.

> This is the origin of the query-prefix convention that BGE users must reproduce at inference time. Getting it wrong silently degrades retrieval.

## Datasets

- **C-MTP**: 16 sources, ~100M pairs, split unlabeled / labeled. The English counterpart is **2× larger** than the Chinese.
- **C-MTEB**: 35 public datasets, 6 task types (Retrieval, STS, Pair Classification, Classification, Re-ranking, Clustering).
- **MTEB** (English evaluation): 56 datasets.

**Two observations worth carrying forward.** First, BGE-large advances prior SOTA (GTE-large) by 1.1 points average — a real but modest margin. Second, and far more useful for constrained hardware: **`bge-small` (24M params, 384-dim) scores 51.68 on retrieval — above `E5-large` (50.56, 326M params) and `OpenAI Ada-002` (49.25, 1536-dim)**, at roughly 1/14 the parameters of the large model. It also beats a 7.1B-parameter SGPT model. The small/large retrieval gap is 51.68 vs 54.29 — **2.6 points for 13.6× the parameters**.

### C-MTEB (Table 2) — scaling

Average rises 58.28 (small) → 62.80 (base) → 63.96 (large). The authors note "our small model is still empirically competitive despite its highly reduced model size, where the average performance is even higher than the large-scale option of many existing models."

### Training-recipe ablations (Table 3, Chinese, 1024-dim)

| Variant | Retrieval | Average |
|---|---|---|
| BGE w.o. pre-train | 62.56 | 58.62 |
| BGE-pretrain (stage 1+2 only) | 63.90 | 59.00 |
| BGE w.o. Instruct | 70.55 | 63.40 |
| **BGE-finetune (full)** | **71.53** | **63.96** |

Stage 3 is where most of the retrieval quality comes from: 63.90 → 71.53. RetroMAE pre-training contributes +0.38 average — small but consistent. Instruction tuning contributes +0.56.

### Batch size (Table 4)

| Batch | 256 | 2,048 | **19,200** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | 57.25 | 60.96 | **63.90** |
| Average | 56.43 | 57.92 | **59.00** |

A +6.65-point retrieval swing from batch size alone, because in-batch negatives *are* the negatives at stage 2. Note Clustering moves the other way (49.52 → 47.73).

Over 20 million HuggingFace downloads as of 2024-04; integrated into LangChain, LlamaIndex and HuggingFace.

## Limitations

- **The paper is Chinese-first.** English results occupy roughly one table and one paragraph; there are no English ablations, no English-specific analysis, and no BEIR-level per-dataset breakdown for English.
- **No domain-specific evaluation.** MTEB is general-domain. Nothing here predicts behaviour on biomedical or clinical text, where specialized encoders (BioBERT, PubMedBERT, MedCPT, BGE-M3 derivatives) may differ substantially.
- **The batch-size result is a resource barrier to reproduction.** A batch of 19,200 is not reachable without substantial multi-GPU infrastructure, so the recipe as published is not re-runnable on commodity hardware — only the released weights are usable.
- **Instruction prefixes are a silent-failure mode.** The paper does not quantify the penalty for omitting them at inference.
- **v1.5 is not this paper.** The widely used `*-v1.5` checkpoints (including `bge-small-en-v1.5`) were released after the initial version with adjusted similarity distribution; the paper describes the family and recipe, not each checkpoint revision.
- **MTEB scores were SOTA "at the time of release" (2023).** The English leaderboard has moved substantially since.

## Relevance to CTRA

1. **It is the provenance for `GraphDense` and the strongest single argument for a small dense encoder.** SPRIG's best non-fused graph variant seeds PPR from `bge-small-en-v1.5`, reaching R@10 0.844 (HotpotQA) / 0.747 (2Wiki) — *above* its BM25-seeded `GraphHybrid`. That result was obtained with a **24M-parameter, 384-dim** model, inside SPRIG's strict **4 GB RAM budget on CPU**.

2. **The size/quality curve is the directly actionable finding.** Retrieval 51.68 (small, 24M) vs 54.29 (large, 326M) is a 2.6-point gap for 13.6× the parameters. This is the same shape as the measured GLiNER result in `research/linearrag-improvements.md` (item 10): **model scale is the lever, and the small model is disproportionately good**. Given the hardware in `notes/bench_rag_hardware/` — GTX 1650 Ti 4 GB, 7 GB RAM, no AVX512-VNNI — a 384-dim embedding is also 2.7× cheaper to store than a 1024-dim one, which bears directly on the RAM wall in `research/rag-alternatives-hybrid-retrieval.md` § 2.

3. **Use the instruction prefix.** Stage-3 training attaches an instruction to the query side only. CTRA's retriever must apply the documented query prefix at search time and *not* to indexed passages, or it silently loses the +0.56 that instruction tuning bought — a bug class that produces no error, only worse rankings.

4. **This is a general-domain model, and that is an open question, not a settled one.** Clinical trial text is dense with drug names, condition codes and eligibility jargon. The unresolved item is whether `bge-small-en-v1.5` or a biomedical encoder wins on TREC-CT — which is exactly what the outstanding retrieval harness (`research/rag-alternatives-hybrid-retrieval.md` § 8) exists to answer. Nothing in this paper settles it.

5. **BGE-small is the recommended starting encoder for CTRA's hybrid retriever** — on the evidence that it is the configuration SPRIG actually validated under a 4 GB cap, and that its retrieval quality beats models an order of magnitude larger.
