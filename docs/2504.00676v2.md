## Abstract

GLiNER-BioMed introduces a domain-adapted suite of efficient models for open biomedical named entity recognition (NER). Unlike conventional NER approaches that rely on fixed taxonomies, GLiNER-BioMed uses natural language labels to enable zero-shot entity recognition of arbitrary biomedical entity types. The approach first distills annotation capabilities of large language models (LLMs) into a smaller, more efficient model to generate high-coverage synthetic biomedical NER datasets. Two GLiNER architectures (uni-encoder and bi-encoder) are trained at multiple scales. Experiments on eight biomedical NER benchmarks demonstrate 5.96% F1-score improvement over the strongest baseline, with effectiveness in both zero-shot and few-shot scenarios.

## Problem Statement

1. **Fixed taxonomy limitations**: Traditional NER models constrained by fixed taxonomies and human annotations struggle to generalize beyond predefined entity types and cannot recognize novel biomedical entities.
2. **Domain complexity**: Biomedical texts present unique challenges — specialized vocabularies, massive entity volumes, and continuous emergence of novel entities (genes, diseases, drugs, biomarkers).
3. **Generalization gap**: Existing approaches (BioBERT, fine-tuned transformers) have limited ability to recognize emerging, domain-specific, or previously unseen biomedical entities.

## Key Contributions

1. **GLiNER-BioMed models**: Domain-adapted GLiNER suite with uni-encoder and bi-encoder variants across three parameter scales (small, base, large), achieving SOTA zero-shot and few-shot biomedical NER.
2. **Synthetic data generation pipeline**: Efficient high-throughput approach using model distillation (OpenBioLLM-70B teacher → OpenBioLLM-8B student) to create 105,000 high-quality synthetic biomedical NER samples with 2.3 million entity mentions covering 640,000 unique entities.
3. **Large-scale evaluation**: Comprehensive evaluation across 8 biomedical NER benchmarks (TAC, CADEC, N2C2, BC5CDR, BioRED, CHIA, BioMed NER, NCBI Disease) with 10,918 passages and 85,959 entity mentions spanning 58 unique biomedical entity types.
4. **Bi-encoder efficiency**: Alternative architecture for large-label scenarios, achieving 39%-568% throughput improvements through label embedding pre-computation and caching.
5. **Open-source release**: All models, synthetic datasets, and training pipelines publicly available.

## Methodology/Architecture

### GLiNER Framework Overview

GLiNER frames NER as a matching problem within a single encoder that jointly represents text and natural language entity labels, enabling lightweight zero-shot recognition without retraining.

### Synthetic Pre-training Data Generation (Section 3.1)

**Corpus Selection**: Aggregated from 5 sources:
- PubMed abstracts (106,982 samples, indexed under pathological conditions, signs, symptoms)
- ClinicalTrials.gov descriptions (76,145 samples)
- DailyMed prescription labels (31,778 samples)
- Biomedical patents — WIPO categories A61P, G16H, A61K (114,609 samples)
- Total curated: ~415k passages

**Quality Filtering**: Heuristic pipeline with lexical/structural metrics (≥6 sentences, ≥10 avg word length, ≤30% non-alphanumeric words, ≥10% lexical diversity, ≥5% stopwords). Stringent criteria for clinical trial treatment regimens.

**Deduplication**: Graph-based strategy using TF-IDF similarity (cosine threshold 0.9), retaining canonical representative with highest average similarity.

**Stratified Sampling**: ~115,000 passages selected with equal representation across all sources.

**Model Distillation & Annotation**:
- Teacher: OpenBioLLM-70B annotates 10,000 initial samples (few-shot, 4 in-context examples)
- Student: OpenBioLLM-8B fine-tuned with LoRA on teacher annotations
- Student then annotates remaining 105,000 samples
- Noun phrase extraction via spaCy constrains candidate entities
- Guided decoding enforces JSON output format

**Final Pre-training Dataset**: 105,000 samples, 2.3M entity mentions, 640k unique entities. Entity mapping to UMLS via exact string matching: 1.5M labels → 2M+ CUIs across 120 of 127 UMLS semantic types, all 15 UMLS semantic groups.

### Post-training Dataset (Section 3.2)

Base dataset (5,000 instances):
- 1,878 manually curated examples (WNUT2017, OntoNotes5, MultiNERD)
- 3,122 synthetic examples (Wikipedia, Llama-3-8B)

Augmented with 14,000 additional synthetic examples using FineWeb dataset and Qwen2.5-72B. Final: 19,000 instances, 337,000 annotated mentions, 12,700 unique labels. WordNet mapping: 195,000 labels across 37 of 45 lexnames, all 26 noun lexnames.

### Model Architectures (Section 3.3)

**Uni-encoder variant**: Single transformer (DeBERTa-v3) jointly encodes text and entity labels.

**Bi-encoder variant**: Two independent encoders:
- Text encoder: DeBERTa-v3
- Label encoder: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (small), bge-small-en-v1.5 (base), bge-base-en-v1.5 (large)
- Label embeddings pre-computed and cached for efficiency

**Training**:
- Pre-training: 20,000 steps, batch size 8, Adam with weight decay 0.01, learning rates 1e-5 (encoder) / 5e-5 (other)
- Post-training: 10,000 steps, batch size 4, learning rates 5e-6 (encoder) / 1e-5 (other)
- 90% train, 10% validation splits

## Results

### Zero-shot Performance (Table 1)

**GLiNER-BioMed-large achieves SOTA**:
- Micro F1: 59.77% (vs. GLiNER-v2.5-large at 53.81%, +5.96 points, p<0.001)
- Outperforms all GLiNER baselines and generative models
- GLiNER-BioMed-small (with 7x fewer parameters) achieves comparable performance to GLiNER-v2.5-large (no significant difference, p>0.05)

**vs. Generative LLMs**:
- Surpasses UniNER-7B by 11.22 F1 points (p<0.001)
- Outperforms PP-UIE-14B by wide margin
- OpenBioLLM-70B: 36.30% F1, OpenBioLLM-8B: 15.95% F1

**Bi-encoder behavior**:
- Small/base: bi-encoder outperforms uni-encoder (+4.40 and +3.94 F1 respectively)
- Large: uni-encoder superior by 4.87 F1 (bi-encoder parameter doubling benefit diminishes at scale)

### Few-shot Performance (Table 2, aggregated over 8 datasets)

GLiNER-BioMed-bi-large consistently outperforms in annotation-constrained settings:
- 10-shot: 70.39% F1 (vs. GLiNER-v2.5-large 65.93%, +4.46 points, p<0.001)
- 20-shot: 73.07% F1 (+3.92 points, p<0.05)
- 50-shot: 76.02% F1 (+2.50 points, p<0.001)
- Full supervision: all converge (~84% F1, no significant difference p>0.05)

### Inference Efficiency (Figure 2)

**Bi-encoder throughput gains** (RTX3090, FP32):
- With dataset-specific labels: 39-63% faster
- With 127 UMLS semantic type labels: 92-568% faster (uni-encoder quadratic O((|SE|+|T|)²) vs. bi-encoder linear O(|T|²))

### Ablation Studies (Table 3)

**Impact of training stages** (GLiNER-v2.5-large baseline):
- Baseline (general-domain only): 53.81% F1
- + Post-training only: 54.80% F1 (modest +0.99)
- Random init + post-training only: 52.38% F1
- Random init + synthetic biomedical pre-training only: 42.10% F1 (high precision 70.08%, low recall 30.09%)
- **Synthetic biomedical + post-training (GLiNER-BioMed-large): 59.77% F1** (balanced precision 56.67%, recall 63.22%)

Synthetic biomedical pre-training essential for domain knowledge; post-training critical for recall and linguistic coverage.

## Limitations

1. **Synthetic data quality**: LLM-distilled annotations may introduce biases or fail to capture complexity of human-annotated data, limiting generalizability.
2. **Evaluation scope**: Benchmark datasets may not represent all biomedical subdomains or linguistic variations (e.g., veterinary medicine, dentistry).
3. **Resource barriers**: Complete pipeline computationally expensive despite optimization efforts; reproducibility challenge in resource-constrained environments.
4. **Qualitative analysis gaps**: Evaluation lacks detailed error analysis, interpretability insights, and directions for improvement.

## Relevance to CTRA

**Critical relevance as AutoCT's NER backbone** (reference implementation [34]):

1. **Zero-shot biomedical entity extraction**: GLiNER-BioMed enables CTRA to dynamically extract entities from clinical trial protocols and literature without retraining, supporting the 16 entity types specified in CTRA's configuration:
   - Drug, Mechanism of action, Drug dosage
   - Disease, Symptom, Adverse event
   - Gene or protein, Biomarker, Cell type, Anatomical structure
   - Clinical endpoint, Therapeutic procedure, Diagnostic test, Patient population
   - Organization, Time period

2. **Confidence thresholding**: GLiNER-BioMed's entity confidence scores align with CTRA's `ner_threshold: 0.4` setting in `src/ctra/config/settings.py`, allowing precision-recall tuning for LinearRAG graph construction.

3. **Biomedical vocabulary handling**: Trained on diverse biomedical corpora (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, ChEMBL, FAERS, patents), GLiNER-BioMed handles the specialized terminology CTRA encounters across its 7 data sources (ClinicalTrials.gov, PubMed, ChEMBL, FAERS/OpenFDA, AACT, PrimeKG, Drugs@FDA).

4. **Few-shot adaptation**: The strong few-shot performance (70.39% F1 with just 10 samples) enables CTRA to adapt to emerging entity types or domain-specific labels with minimal retraining.

5. **Inference efficiency**: Bi-encoder architecture's dramatic speedups (up to 568%) are valuable for CTRA's high-throughput RAG pipeline, where NER is applied to thousands of retrieved passages per query.

6. **Integration with spaCy**: GLiNER-BioMed via `gliner-spacy` natively integrates with CTRA's NER pipeline in `src/ctra/rag/ner_config.py`, which already uses `ner_model: "gliner_bio"` and default configuration.
