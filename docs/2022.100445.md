## Abstract

Clinical trials are crucial for drug development but often face uncertain outcomes due to safety, efficacy, or patient-recruitment problems. The authors propose the Hierarchical Interaction Network (HINT) to predict clinical trial outcomes. HINT first encodes multi-modal data (drug molecule, target disease, trial eligibility criteria) into embeddings. Then it trains knowledge-embedding modules using drug pharmacokinetic and historical trial data. Finally, a hierarchical interaction graph connects all embeddings to capture their interactions and predict trial outcomes. HINT was trained and validated on 1,160 phase I trials, 4,449 phase II trials, and 3,436 phase III trials. It obtained 0.665, 0.620, and 0.847 F1 scores on separate test sets of 627 phase I, 1,653 phase II, and 1,140 phase III trials, respectively. HINT significantly outperforms the best baseline method on most metrics.

## Problem Statement

Clinical trials are extremely expensive (up to hundreds of millions of dollars), take multiple years, and have a low success probability. Many factors -- drug inefficacy, safety issues, poor protocol design -- can cause failure. The paper addresses the question: **Can we predict the trial outcome (binary success/failure in meeting primary endpoints) before the trial starts, using an in silico approach?**

Existing approaches suffered from: (1) lack of benchmark data, (2) limited task definitions and study scope (focusing on individual components or subsets of diseases), (3) limited features used for prediction, and (4) ignoring complex relations among trial components.

## Key Contributions

1. **Problem formulation:** Formally defined a general clinical-trial-outcome prediction task that models drug safety, treatment efficacy, trial recruitment risk, and generalizes across a wide range of drugs and disease indications.
2. **Benchmark dataset (TOP):** Created and released the Trial Outcome Prediction benchmark -- 17,538 clinical trials with 13,880 small-molecule drugs and 5,335 diseases, including drug molecules, disease codes, eligibility criteria, and binary outcome labels.
3. **Method (HINT):** Designed a graph neural network that explicitly simulates different clinical trial components and their interaction relations using a hierarchical interaction graph with a dynamic attentive GNN.
4. **Results:** Achieved state-of-the-art performance (F1 of 0.665 / 0.620 / 0.847 for phases I / II / III) with statistically significant improvements over the best baseline (COMPOSE) on most metrics.

## Methodology / Architecture

### Overview

HINT is an end-to-end neural network pipeline with two main stages:

1. **Trial interaction graph construction** -- builds a hierarchical graph connecting all input data sources
2. **Dynamic attentive graph neural network** -- predicts trial outcomes by propagating information through the graph

### Input Encoding (Tier 1: Input Nodes)

- **Drug molecule embedding (h_m):** Drug SMILES strings are converted to molecular graphs and embedded using a Message Passing Neural Network (MPNN). For trials with multiple drugs, embeddings are averaged. An imputation module handles missing molecule data.
- **Disease embedding (h_d):** ICD-10 codes and disease descriptions are embedded using the GRAM (Graph-based Attention Model) which leverages medical ontology hierarchies.
- **Eligibility criteria embedding (h_p):** Inclusion/exclusion criteria sentences are embedded using Clinical-BERT, a domain-specific BERT model.

### External Knowledge Pre-training (Tier 2: Knowledge Nodes)

- **ADMET embeddings (h_A, h_D, h_M, h_E, h_T):** Five pharmacokinetic property models (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, Toxicity) are pre-trained on wet lab data from MoleculeNet and used to augment drug embeddings.
- **Disease risk embedding (h_R):** Pre-trained on historical success rates for target diseases from ClinicalTrials.gov.

### Aggregation (Tier 3: Aggregation Nodes)

- **Pharmacokinetics (PK) node:** Aggregates all five ADMET embeddings.
- **Interaction node:** Models the interaction among drug molecule, disease, and eligibility criteria.
- **Augmented interaction node:** Combines disease risk with the interaction embedding.

### Prediction (Tier 4)

- **Trial prediction node:** Summarizes PK and augmented interaction information.
- A **dynamic attentive GCN** enhances node representations via message passing with a learned attention matrix V that weights edge importance.
- Final prediction uses sigmoid on the trial prediction node's last-layer representation, trained with binary cross-entropy loss.

The graph has K=13 nodes total. The model is trained end-to-end.

## Datasets

### Primary Dataset: TOP (Trial Outcome Prediction)

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Total trials | 17,538 |
| Filtered by date (used in experiments) | 12,465 |
| Unique drugs | 13,880 (small molecules) |
| Unique diseases | 5,335 |
| Success rate | 57.0% (9,999 successes / 7,539 failures) |

**Phase breakdown (train/valid/test):**

| Phase | Train | Valid | Test | Avg patients/trial |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase I | 1,044 | 116 | 627 | 45 |
| Phase II | 4,004 | 445 | 1,653 | 183 |
| Phase III | 3,092 | 344 | 1,140 | 1,418 |

- **Time split:** Train/validation completion dates before Jan 1, 2014; test start dates after Jan 1, 2014.
- **Sources:** ClinicalTrials.gov (369,700 raw trials), DrugBank (molecule structures), ICD-10 codes, CCS codes.
- **Labels:** Manually curated by an internal IQVIA team.

### Auxiliary Datasets

- **Pharmacokinetics (ADMET):** 640 (absorption), 1,593 (distribution), 15,020 (metabolism), 15,982 (excretion), 24,576 (toxicity) drug entries from MoleculeNet.
- **Disease risk:** 16,356 disease combinations with historical success rates.

### Disease Subgroups Evaluated

- Neoplasm (19.4%), Respiratory (8.4%), Digestive (12.7%), Nervous system (12.0%), Others

## Results

### Phase-Level Outcome Predictions (F1 scores, 30 independent runs)

| Method | Phase I F1 | Phase II F1 | Phase III F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | 0.604 | 0.555 | 0.698 |
| Random Forest | 0.621 | 0.563 | 0.686 |
| XGBoost | 0.621 | 0.570 | 0.696 |
| kNN+RF | 0.625 | 0.590 | 0.698 |
| FFNN | 0.634 | 0.599 | 0.748 |
| DeepEnroll | 0.648 | 0.598 | 0.786 |
| COMPOSE | 0.658 | 0.597 | 0.792 |
| **HINT** | **0.665** | **0.620** | **0.847** |

- HINT achieves 1.0%, 3.9%, and 7.0% relative F1 improvement over COMPOSE for phases I, II, and III.
- Deep learning methods significantly outperform classical ML across all phases.
- Phase III is easiest to predict; phases I and II are more challenging.

### Disease Subgroup Results (Phase III)

| Cohort | PR-AUC | F1 | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neoplasm | 0.604 | 0.585 | 0.595 |
| Respiratory | 0.860 | 0.867 | 0.805 |
| Digestive | 0.849 | 0.858 | 0.813 |
| Nervous system | 0.750 | 0.786 | 0.728 |
| All diseases | 0.811 | 0.847 | 0.723 |

- Neoplasm trials are the hardest to predict; respiratory trials are the easiest.

### Case Studies

Notable correct predictions include: Entresto for heart failure (predicted failure, p=0.476), Fevipiprant for asthma (predicted failure, p=0.352), Sitagliptin for diabetes (predicted success, p=0.742), and Aflibercept for glaucoma (predicted success, p=0.854).

## Limitations

1. **Limited trial types:** Only handles interventional trials with small molecules; does not cover medical devices, biologics, or surgery trials.
2. **Rare diseases:** Insufficient historical trial data for low-prevalence diseases leads to worse predictions; unseen disease codes in the test set are more likely to be mispredicted.
3. **Limited interpretability:** The complex interaction patterns in the GNN make predictions difficult to explain, though some intermediate analysis is provided.
4. **Binary outcome labels:** Only predicts success/failure; does not classify specific failure reasons (e.g., safety vs. efficacy vs. recruitment).
5. **Look-ahead bias:** Pre-trained embedding modules (ADMET, trial risk encoders) may use data that was not available at the time-split cutoff (Jan 1, 2014), potentially introducing subtle information leakage.

## Relevance to CTRA

HINT is highly relevant to the CTRA project in several ways:

- **Shared problem domain:** Both HINT and CTRA deal with predicting clinical trial outcomes before the trial completes. While CTRA predicts protocol uninformativeness (i.e., whether a trial protocol will yield useful information), HINT predicts binary success/failure in meeting primary endpoints. The feature spaces overlap significantly (drug molecules, disease codes, eligibility criteria, protocol design elements).
- **Benchmark and data pipeline:** The TOP benchmark dataset and the data curation pipeline from ClinicalTrials.gov provide a reusable foundation. CTRA could leverage the same data sources and filtering logic, potentially extending them with additional protocol quality features.
- **Multi-modal architecture:** HINT's approach of encoding heterogeneous trial components (molecules, diseases, eligibility criteria) into a unified graph representation is architecturally instructive. CTRA could adapt or extend the hierarchical interaction graph to incorporate protocol quality signals.
- **Eligibility criteria encoding:** HINT's use of Clinical-BERT for eligibility criteria embedding is directly applicable to CTRA, since protocol text quality is a key predictor of uninformativeness.
- **Limitations as opportunities:** HINT's acknowledged limitations (binary labels, no protocol quality assessment, no failure-reason classification) are precisely the gaps that CTRA aims to fill. CTRA's focus on protocol uninformativeness can be seen as predicting one specific failure mode that HINT does not distinguish.
- **Baseline for comparison:** HINT's results provide a strong baseline against which CTRA models can be benchmarked, particularly if CTRA reuses the TOP dataset.
